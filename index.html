The Free Game That Wasn't

A three o'clock Florida storm, a bored family on the couch, and a bright new game on the big TV that costs nothing to play. Castle Kingdoms rains gold coins, flashes a countdown clock, and dares you not to miss a day.

Chase is all in. Ash starts asking the questions nobody else is asking. And one by one the Wilders figure out the oldest trick there is — dressed up in new colors.

The lesson

“Nothing is free. Somebody always pays. If you can't see who — look in the mirror.”

The cast

Meet the Wilders

A family of big ideas and strong opinions — and one 82-pound bulldog who's always exactly where he shouldn't be.

G
Grant
Dad · builds his own thing

Problem-solver, business owner, and the family's funny guy. He can build, fix, or figure out almost anything — and once he's sure he's right, good luck changing his mind.

G
Gemma
Mom · runs her own brokerage

Smart, confident, generous, and nobody's fool. She says what she thinks even when nobody wants to hear it, and she's usually three steps ahead. Sometimes her confidence gets there first.

A

The big sister · 14

Independent, outspoken, and always one step ahead — or so she thinks. Addie has an opinion about everything and will challenge anyone, especially her brothers.

C
Chase
The big dreamer · 12

Athletic, competitive, charming, and full of ideas. Chase doesn't just dream big — he makes things happen and can talk almost anyone into an adventure. Sometimes his excitement runs ahead of his judgment.

A
Ash
The question master · 10

Curious, confident, funny, and the family's stock-chart expert. Ash asks the questions nobody else thinks to ask and notices what everybody else misses. Don't underestimate the youngest.

M
Moosie
The family bulldog

Eighty-two pounds of wrinkles, snoring, and stubbornness — always exactly where he shouldn't be, especially when food is involved. Never says a word, always part of the action.

The bigger picture

Book one of many.

The Wilders live on a few acres outside Tampa, where the afternoon storms roll in at three and the real world keeps handing kids lessons school never gets to.

Each book takes on one of those lessons — the "free" game that hooks you, the deal that's too good, owning versus renting your whole life, who really profits when something goes viral. A growing library for parents who want their kids to see clearly.
